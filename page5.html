What is ANOVA?

Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) is a statistical method used to test if there are significant differences between the means of three or more independent groups (treatments). If you only have two groups, you would use a t-test. For three or more, we use the F-test in ANOVA.

The Standard Class Procedure (Step-by-Step)

To get full marks in an AGE 301 ANOVA question, follow this exact sequence of procedures:

Step 1: State the Hypothesis

Always start by defining your Null (H0) and Alternative (H1) hypotheses.

  • H0: μ1 = μ2 = μ3 = ... = μk (There is no significant difference between the treatment means).
  • H1: At least one treatment mean is significantly different.

Step 2: Calculate the Mean and Totals of Each Treatment

Before jumping into complex formulas, calculate the basic sums:

  • Find the sum of each treatment group (T1, T2, T3, etc.).
  • Calculate the mean of each treatment (if requested).
  • Find the Grand Total (G): The sum of ALL observations in the experiment.
  • Find the sum of all squared individual observations (ΣX²).

Step 3: The Correction Factor (CF)

This is the anchor for all your Sum of Squares calculations. Do not round this number off too early!

CF = G² / N

Where G is the Grand Total and N is the total number of observations across all groups.

Step 4: Calculate the Sum of Squares (SS)

You need to calculate three different sums of squares:

  • Total Sum of Squares (SSTotal):
    SSTotal = ΣX² - CF
  • Treatment Sum of Squares (SSTreatment):
    SSTreatment = [ (ΣTi²) / n ] - CF
    (Square each treatment total, add them up, divide by the number of observations in a single treatment 'n', then subtract CF).
  • Error Sum of Squares (SSError):
    SSError = SSTotal - SSTreatment

Step 5 & 6: Degrees of Freedom (DF) and Mean Squares (MS)

Fill these directly into your ANOVA table. Mean Square is simply the Sum of Squares divided by its corresponding Degree of Freedom.

MSTreatment = SSTreatment / DFTreatment
MSError = SSError / DFError

Step 7: The F-Calculated

This is the final test statistic you are looking for.

Fcalc = MSTreatment / MSError

The ANOVA Table Structure

Your final presentation should always look exactly like this:

Source of Variation Sum of Squares (SS) Degree of Freedom (DF) Mean Square (MS) F-Calculated
Treatment SSTreatment k - 1 MSTreatment MSTrt / MSErr
Error SSError N - k MSError
Total SSTotal N - 1

Note: 'k' is the number of treatments, and 'N' is the total number of observations.

How to Look Up the F-Table and Make a Decision

To know if your calculated F-value is significant, you must compare it to the Tabulated F-value from the statistical tables provided in the exam.

  1. Identify your Alpha (α): This is the significance level. Usually, it is 0.05 (5%) unless stated otherwise. Go to the page in your booklet for α = 0.05.
  2. Identify v1 (Numerator DF): This is your DFTreatment (k - 1). Look across the top row of the table for this number.
  3. Identify v2 (Denominator DF): This is your DFError (N - k). Look down the first column on the left side of the table for this number.
  4. Find the Intersection: Where the v1 column and v2 row intersect is your Ftabulated (or F-critical).
The Golden Rule for the Conclusion:
If Fcalculated > Ftabulated, you REJECT the Null Hypothesis (H0) and conclude that there IS a significant difference between the treatments.

If Fcalculated ≤ Ftabulated, you ACCEPT (fail to reject) the Null Hypothesis and conclude that there is NO significant difference.
